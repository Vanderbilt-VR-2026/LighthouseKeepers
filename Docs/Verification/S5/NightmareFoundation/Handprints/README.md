# Day 2 stair handprints (#144)

The rail renderers share larger shell meshes. The old projection cast a metre above each segment, so it could hit a different floor and stretch one handprint up to 0.87 m. The projection now casts only through the corresponding renderer's bounds and rejects a discontinuity from the local rail surface. It uses the same material and adds no gameplay collider or network data.

The initial controlled Editor check exposed the out-of-bounds marks. After the fix, a muted Play Mode fixture found all six marks, confirmed their visibility after the child's descent, verified no added colliders, and checked every generated vertex is inside its own rail's bounds and within 6 mm of the surface: **6/6 checks passed** (`results/after/checks.txt`). The initial failing run and measured pre-fix bounds are preserved in `results/before/`. The final surface tolerance is 6 mm to accommodate the 1.5 mm normal offset on sloped bevels; the added rail-bounds assertion catches the original much larger displacement. I looked at `images/wet-handprint.png`, captured under the actual flashlight with the Quest URP asset in the Editor; the mark reads against the rail at close range. 40 focused EditMode checks also passed (NightmareIntegration 5, ScareSequence 22, SurvivalMode 10, QualityBaseline 3). The camera and flashlight were placed by the fixture, so this does not establish reach, typical player visibility or on-device rendering/performance.

To reproduce: open Unity 6000.3.23f1, set `EditorUtility.audioMasterMute=true`, run `python3 artifacts/s5/scare-scenes/start.py`, then use Pipeline `run_script` to invoke `HandprintProbe.Begin` with a fresh absolute output path.
