## 1. The pitch

Autumn, 1923. The port city of **Saltmere** lives on one deep-water approach, the **Kettle Passage**, past the black reef called **the Kettles**. Coal, mail, fish and passengers all come through it; going around the cape takes two days. On the Kettles stands **Kettle Rock Light**.

Every other station of the Saltmere Board of Lights has one keeper. Kettle Rock has **two, always**. The Board blames the machinery: diesel engine, pumps, power panel and radio that one person cannot run in a storm. That is true. It is not the reason.

Six weeks ago both keepers vanished: **Walter Brennick**, keeper for nineteen years, and junior **Tom Merrow**, twenty-four. Bunks still made, tea tins still on the shelf, logbooks stopping mid-sentence. The Board calls it a drowning and sends two replacements for five nights while a storm cuts the Rock off from the city.

The dependable, dry, tired radio voice is **Elias Merrow**, harbor master and Tom's father. His instructions are always correct; what he withholds is why the rule exists. The pipe is torn, not rusted; the tank drained, with handprints on the cap. Something patient comes out of the sea and wants the light out. It can take only someone **alone in the dark**.

The central mystery is **why two keepers?** Nothing can take a keeper who is being watched. The rule was written in the first keeper's blood in 1874; the machinery excuse was added in 1919. Walter broke it once to save a ship, costing both men. On the final night the biggest ship is inbound and everything fails: split up for speed, or keep each other. Both solutions must be winnable. Every repair resists the Drowned's darkness; every saved ship is a life it does not get.

## 2. Truth, history and motives

### The Drowned

The sea off the Kettles keeps what it drowns. Two centuries of wrecks became one patient hunger: everyone the reef took, worn as one thing. It wants ships; every wreck adds to it; the light starves it.

1. **TAKE requires unwitnessed AND dark.** No other human keeper or Owen can see the victim; no powered room lamp and no nearby HIGH lantern protects them. Watched or lit keepers can be frightened/stalked, never taken. Floor membership alone is not line of sight.
2. Elsewhere it acts on **things only when nobody watches**: torn pipe, drained fuel, yanked cable, fouled lens. Two faults on different floors tempt separation.
3. Light it did not make hurts it. It crosses lit rooms briefly, steaming, but never lingers. LOW lantern slows it; HIGH held on it for 2 s drives it back ~20 s (15% oil). A shining beacon keeps it out of F4: it attacks power below or glass outside. One flare per night on the House bench sends it to sea ~60 s.
4. The F2 storm bell called boats home for two hundred years. The drowned died hearing it without reaching it. Once per night it drives the entity out ~45 s.
5. Lost ships make the next night's entity faster and more willing to sabotage. Speech is quiet, rare, intimate, observant, only to someone alone. Never imitates live players or invents system messages.

One physical body/controller at a time; forms switch only unseen. Physical wet bodies, opaque, no white glow/transparency. Dragging cloth, wet bare-foot slaps/prints, water in the throat. Mostly believable motion punctuated by brief impossible movement: a three-metre step, too-far head turn, disappearance between lightning flashes.

| Form | Identity | Role and appearance |
|---|---|---|
| Old woman | Margaret Hollin, first keeper's widow, taken 1874 | Saboteur; soaked black dress with keeper's oilskin, flattened grey hair. Knows every machine; prints on tank/glass; walking away at the light's edge. |
| Child | Lottie Vance, 7, lost on brig Corrie Dee, 1891 | Lure; wet pinafore, hair over face, unnaturally still close up. Apparitions only. Hums the first line of Eternal Father, Strong to Save. |
| Tall figure | Walter Brennick, taken six weeks ago | Hunter; patched oilskin and pipe, 2.3 m, gaunt long arms, bowed under beams. Newest face still fitting him; knows stairs and hiding places. |

### Chronology

- **1868:** barque *Hesper*, forty dead on the Kettles; Saltmere petitions for a light.
- **1872:** oil lamp lit, Josiah Hollin and wife Margaret keep it.
- **October 1873:** Josiah drowns repairing the landing; Margaret requests help, none arrives before winter.
- **February 1874:** after eleven weeks alone Margaret writes: “It stands where the light stops. It wears Josiah's coat now.” She vanishes; light goes out; *Ellen Ward* wrecks; relief boat sent that night is lost.
- **1874 Rule 14:** “Kettle Rock shall be kept by two keepers at all hours. Neither keeper shall be left alone in darkness.” Sealed clause: “Should one keeper be taken, the harbor office shall send no relief before dawn.” Boats sent at night never came back.
- **9 November 1891:** lamp fire, light out forty minutes; *Corrie Dee*, thirty-one dead, Lottie Vance seven, body never found.
- **1904:** Walter becomes senior keeper. Nineteen years of obeying the rule with successive juniors. He paints NEVER ALONE IN THE DARK above the F2 door.
- **1916:** Elias becomes harbor master and countersigns the sealed clause.
- **1919:** diesel/electric beacon/panel/radio installed under Elias. Public Rule 14 amended to blame machinery. Walter: “It has learned the engine.” Darkness now comes from inside.
- **Spring 1923:** Tom joins; Elias obtained “the safest posting on the coast — there's always two of you.”
- **August 1923:** tank drained twice; Walter records small hand marks.
- **14 September, 02:10:** storm; radio wire torn, Tom splices it. *Lucy Gale*, forty aboard, approaches; tank drained. Elias: “Light her through, whatever it takes.” Walter sends Tom up and goes alone below: “A ship's a ship.” Engine starts, ship passes, Walter does not return. Watch stops **02:31**. Ship clear **02:48**.
- **15–16 September:** Tom keeps the light alone: “W. came back. Wet through. Stands at the bottom of the stairs where the lamp stops. Won't come in. He's too tall.” At **03:20** Tom calls: “Da, he's on the stairs. It's Walter but it's not. Send the boat.” Elias obeys the clause, fears losing a crew too, sends none. Dark **03:31**, relit **03:52** by Tom. Empty Rock at dawn.
- **Late October 1923:** Board sends replacements without the truth. Elias insists on two; solo player paired with **Owen Teague**, deaf ex-signalman, sharpest eyes on the coast. Whispers do not lure him.

Saltmere needs the Passage or its port dies in a season. Board pays triple, calls losses “drowned in the course of duty.” No pair staying together has been taken in fifty-one years. Board knows; town half-knows; storm-night candles in windows “for the Kettle.”

Elias (58): good practical man who obeyed a rule over his son, wants keepers/ships safe, needs to confess. Plain short nautical speech, dry humor, never theatrical. Walter (61): kind, steady, superstitious and right; sacrificed his own rule for forty strangers; written evidence only. Tom (24): brave, cheerful, cocky, loves his father; last act relighting; written only. Margaret: first keeper, eleven weeks alone, log origin of rule. Owen (44): deafened at Jutland 1916, calm, observant, dry slate humor, never betrayal. Drowned never explains itself.

## 3. Five nights

### Night 1 — The Leak

Belief: well-paid hard job; predecessors drowned; rule about machinery. Question: two mugs/bunks/names crossed off, 1874 rule versus 1919 engine. Torn F1 joint: wrench three bolts, power pump, learn panel. **Mary Ellen**, collier, safe preview. Wet bare prints from pipe to ajar banging sea door; ambiguous, nothing else. No sightings/voice during night. Optional orders, roster, thank-you letter, photo, memorial, postcard. HM warm professional reassurance. Off-screen initial pipe sabotage. Recovery: pump drains, ship horn. Dawn only: one lightning-lit second of tall figure on far rocks, unremarked.

### Night 2 — Blackout

Question: who drained tank, why small prints? Generator coughs/dies mid-watch; introduce lantern. Refuel → prime → choke → pull, then scarce-power choice while **Northern Star** needs beacon. Co-op gauge read at panel/other existing clue location. First sighting: Margaret walks from tank at lantern's edge, disappears into dark. Later child at dark stair foot and wet doll on F3, 2–3 apparitions, no hunt. Walter engine log, lantern-readable lintel, unsent letter. HM denies prints too fast, remembers Walter. One isolated-dark whisper about engine. Engine restoration/lights floor by floor should be best sound in game. Morning doll dry on House bench.

### Night 3 — The Warning

Question: what does it do to people, why hide? Yanked F2 cable blacks out F1/F2; join at junction box. Broadcast code House telegram → F4 radio; solo Owen fetches/slates. **Halcyon**, **Saint Brendan**. First hunt: dips/drips/wet breathing, silence, tall figure on stairs; HM tells hiding; searches/passes/leaves. Margaret log under bunk while hiding, wardrobe scratches, KEEP TOGETHER telegram. HM recognizes August sequence, admits machinery excuse. Quiet cupboard threat. Restore circuit, reunite, ship horn. Morning wet patched coat on F2 hook.

### Night 4 — Everything Breaks

Question: what happened to Walter/Tom? Pipe/fuel sabotage, then radio wire, lens, panel overload. **Gannet**, **Wexford Lass** close. Bearing at radio → gallery wheel. Flare enters play. Lens outside handprints then Margaret's hand during ship window. Two hunts. Solo false three-even knocks while Owen visibly beside you (his is two short, one long), slate NOT ME. Logs of Lucy Gale, pocket watch 02:31, “too tall.” HM says Tom is his boy; “If you see Walter, it's not Walter.” Faster hunter targets isolated keeper, repeats “A ship's a ship, boy.” Recovery ship clears, stage eases/music resolves. Morning Tom's grease print appears on wheel.

### Night 5 — The Long Night

Question: will you go down alone when light needs you? **Kittiwake**, **Albion Rose**, finale passenger steamer **Providence (212 aboard)**. During finale tank drained + lens smeared + hunter; all split clues/valve. Split fast or go together safely (down/restart/up/wipe/hold), **both must win**; flare/bell escape each. Child lures, Margaret glass, Walter hunts. Last isolated words “He stood in the light until it went out.” Last lens note and sealed clause. Elias confesses in four calm beats, never in danger: ordered whatever it takes, did not send Tom a boat, will not do it again, keep each other first. Solo Owen payoff. Success: Providence lit end to end, long horn; grey-gold dawn, entity walks into sea as light reaches it; Elias comes himself, city candle windows, rescue/logbook/skippable credits, player always free/no forced camera. Wreck or keeper lost: matching failure/retry watch.

## 4. Encounter rules and vocabulary

Host authoritative. Never first 60 s, never hunt in first-time tutorial. New problems >=15 s apart, 10–20 s breather after fix. Existing unresolved jobs may coexist. Telegraph drip, lamp dip, wet breathing, thinning music, 2–3 s near silence. About 60% suspicious sounds have discoverable real sources (knocking pipe, banging door, skylight rain, partner feet); normal sounds often stay normal.

| Event | Type / schedule | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| E1 prints sabotage→exit | Evidence each sabotage | Fade 20 s after fix |
| E2 moved sheet/mug/puddle/doll | N2+, room unvisited >60 s | Max 2/night |
| E3 rocks lightning figure | N1 dawn, N3, N5 dawn | Once/night |
| E4 Margaret leaves tank | N2 first blackout | Scripted once |
| E5 wet shape crosses doorway | N2+ blackouts | 1–2/night |
| E6 child dark stair bottom | N2/N5, gone when lantern reaches | Once/night |
| E7 tall shape bent under beams | N3+ before hunts | Telegraph only |
| E8 face at F3 window | N3 lightning | Once/game, very brief |
| E9 prints outside lens | N4/N5 window | Scripted |
| E10 doorway lunge into lantern pool then gone | N3/N5 after silence | Max 1/night, >=0.5 m from face |
| E11 stalk→chase→search→leave | N3 once, N4 twice, N5 | Escape within ~10 s |
| E12 wrong knock | Solo N4, Owen beside player | Once/game |
| E13 hymn from below | N2+ | Once/night |

| Protection | Effect |
|---|---|
| Powered lamps | Can't be taken; entity crosses quickly steaming, cannot linger |
| LOW lantern, 2.5 m | Slows at pool edge, not full safety |
| HIGH for 2 s | Repel ~20 s, consumes 15% oil; nearby HIGH protects keeper |
| Beacon shining | Entity cannot enter F4 |
| One bench flare/night | Flees sea ~60 s; only weapon |
| One F2 bell/night | Leaves tower ~45 s |
| Partner's actual sight | Cannot be taken |

Scares Off: no body/voice, off-screen sabotage remains. Low: evidence/apparitions only, no hunt/lunge. Full: all. No strobe, no forced head motion, no deafening stingers, critical sound also visual. Suspense rather than constant interruption.

## 5. Owen Teague — solo companion [NEW]

One silent ex-Navy keeper, never in two-human co-op. No speech recognition, works offline. Always two keepers in fiction after Owen ships. Never hurt/taken, no escort objective/betrayal. No voice actor: slate, gestures, knocks. Nod/shake/point/palm/beckon. Two short + one long knock (Morse U, “You are running into danger” Easter egg). Knock when losing sight in dark. Slate <=5 words readable 2 m: FUEL GAUGE: 1/4; CODE 4-7-1 (runtime code, not fixed); BEARING 210; STAY IN THE LIGHT; I'M HERE; NOT ME; GOOD WORK.

Point+A or Watch Menu: FOLLOW ME / WAIT HERE / HELP HERE / WORK THIS STATION. Can perform second valve role, read/write split clues, hand pump, hold lantern, carry oil can. Cannot wrench/start engine/radio/beacon/flare. Can be left behind; no sight = alone. Companion must use the same witness rule as humans. Wrong knock fair because he is visibly beside player and writes NOT ME.

## 6. Environmental story catalogue [NEW]

Paper/brass grab/raise/read, texture text, <20 s, no added caption UI. Optional lore never gates winning. **Only telegram #15 essential**, alongside current station signage. Existing positions win.

1. **Standing orders, N1, House beside board:** “KETTLE ROCK LIGHT — STANDING ORDERS. Rule 14: This station shall be kept by TWO keepers at all hours. The machinery cannot be worked single-handed in weather. — Saltmere Board of Lights, 1874 (amended 1919).” Practical→anachronistic cover story.
2. **Duty roster, N1, House door:** KEEPERS — W. BRENNICK / T. MERROW crossed through; RELIEF KEEPERS (2) fresh. Turnover→missing pair.
3. **Lucy Gale letter, N1, job board:** “To the keepers of Kettle Rock — the Lucy Gale and her forty souls thank you for the night of 14th September. Capt. R. Hale.” Heroic night→Walter's sacrifice.
4. **Saltmere postcard, N1, shelf:** harbor view, “Candle's in the window for you both. Keep a light for the Kettle. — Mum”. Homely→dawn city candles.
5. **Photo, N1, above F3 bunk:** tall Walter, pipe, oilskin pale left-shoulder patch; grinning Tom on gallery. Back “Me and the old man. Da says smile. — T.” Later match photo/coat across co-op floors; Da=Elias.
6. **Corrie Dee plate, N1, F1 valve:** “IN MEMORY OF THE 31 LOST WITH THE BRIG CORRIE DEE, 9 NOV 1891, INCLUDING LOTTIE VANCE, AGED 7.” Salt-green brass. History→child.
7. **Engine plate, N1, generator:** “DIESEL GENERATING SET — INSTALLED 1919 — E. MERROW, HARBOR MASTER, SUPERVISING”. Elias has known for years.
8. **Walter engine log, N2, generator clipboard:** “3 Aug 1923 — Tank drained overnight. No leak. Oily hand marks on the cap. Small hand. — W.B. / 19 Aug — Again. Taught the boy the engine. Told him: never come down here alone after dark. He laughed.”
9. **Lintel, N2, F2 door:** NEVER ALONE IN THE DARK, faded beneath FUEL — 2 MEN TO PRIME, lantern-readable dark decal. Walter rule under Board explanation; does not change working primer input.
10. **Tom unsent letter, N2, F3 bunk shelf:** “Da — Walter says it's two of us because of the engine. That's not why, is it? He won't go below after dark without me. Nor me without him. I'm not scared. I'm only asking. — Tom”. Father never answered.
11. **Wet rag doll L.V.:** F3 N2 soaked; House bench N3 morning dry; stair bottom N5. Something moves it; Lottie.
12. **Patched oilskin, N3 morning+, F2 door hook:** soaking/dripping, pale left shoulder patch matches photo. Walter returned wearing it.
13. **Margaret page, N3, under F3 mattress:** “4th February 1874. Eleven weeks alone. The lamp holds it off, but I cannot watch the lamp and the door at once. It stands where the light stops. It wears Josiah's coat now. — M.H.” Visible from hiding, origin of rule/forms.
14. **Wardrobe interior, N3:** tally, W.B., T.M., “It walked past twice. Hold your breath. —T”. Diegetic hiding instruction, Tom hid here.
15. **Telegram, N3/N5, House desk:** “SALTMERE HARBOR OFFICE TO KETTLE ROCK — BROADCAST CODE TONIGHT {CODE} — REPORT ON THE HOUR — STOP KEEP TOGETHER STOP — E.M.” Existing runtime code; co-op relay, Owen in solo. Essential, readable every attempt, no optional-lore gate.
16. **Tom radio log, N4, F4 console:** “14 Sept. 2:10 — Lucy Gale sighted. HM: light her through whatever it takes. W. going below alone. I said no. He said a ship's a ship. / 2:48 — Lucy Gale clear. W. not back.” Connect later antagonist line.
17. **Walter watch, N4, F2 half under engine:** stopped **2:31**, engraved “W.B. — 20 years — come home. M.E.” Mary Ellen his wife, same name as N1 ship.
18. **Tom last entries:** N4 F4 “15 Sept — W. came back. Wet through. Stands at the bottom of the stairs where the lamp stops. Won't come in. He's too tall.” N5 lens access “3:52. Light's lit again. It came up the stairs wearing Walter. I won't be alone in the dark. I'll stand in the light till Da's boat comes. — T.” Emotional explanation of relighting.
19. **Sealed clause, N5, under House flare case:** “CONFIDENTIAL. Rule 14, second clause: Should one keeper be taken, the harbor office shall send NO relief before dawn. Boats sent in darkness are not recovered. — Board of Lights, 1874. Countersigned: E. Merrow, 1916.” Elias confesses even if not read.

Other Easter eggs: eleven-week tally on F3 bunk, hymn “for those in peril on the sea”, 1874 vs1919 dates, KEEP TOGETHER, Morse U. No collectible screen needed for deadline.
