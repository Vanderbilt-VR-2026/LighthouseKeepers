# Lighthouse Keepers — team design document

## Questions for the team

### 1. When should keepers swap jobs, and how should they know whose action is needed next?


**Team answer:**

We think that keepers should have to swap jobs as needed to handle the tasks. Users won't be able to do all tasks at once, so they need to prioritize what is important at that moment.


### 2. What should a new player understand before choosing Host/Join, readying and beginning?



**Team answer:**

They will feel the environment in the lobby, but the idea is that they can learn on the fly while playing. Obviously, players will get a lot better as they play more and more levels, but I think this will provide a solid balance for new and experienced players.


### 3. How should the endangered keeper and rescuer recognize danger, the rescue target and a successful catch?


**Team answer:**

They should feel the water creeping in and the tension that this creates. Our goal for the game is that the flood is constant and seemingly inescapable so that the players are always worried about the next task and surviving.


### 4. What should each refuge feel like, and what observations would justify adjusting its timing?



**Team answer:**
A refuge should be a short moment of calm before reentering the persistent danger. We envision players taking a few moments to move their inventory, plan for the next area, and just take a breath. However, even when players are in a refuge, they should still feel the impending doom to come in the next rooms, which is probably best shown through the walls slowly cracking and such.


### 5. What should players learn after losing pump support, choosing a wrong beacon switch or failing a rescue?



**Team answer:**

They will learn through outcomes as the flood will rise even more and pipes will begin to leak. There will be some guides to see where interactions such as pipes need to happen, but through the rich visuals it will be simple for the players to know what needs to be addressed.

### 6. Which cues must remain noticeable during the storm, and what can be quieter or dimmer?



**Team answer:**

We want all the cues to be noticeable during the storm, but the stress of the rising flood and tasks will lead to them missing many of them. With the time crunch environment, it can be hard for players to prioritize and that is what will make the game difficult.

### 7. What should the keepers see or hear when a route opens, and when both finally reach the restored beacon?



**Team answer:**

They should see a physical route open and a temporary pause with the flood. Additionally, there should be obvious audio effects to accompany this. Once they reach the final beacon, they should be rewarded with a final moment of calm and celebration along with their collective celebration that they survived. We visualize it being like someone finishing a long endurance race, pride on what they accomplished.

### 8. Which information should both keepers see, and which should one keeper relay to the other?


**Team answer:**

Keepers can hypothetically see everything, but they can only be in one place at once so they need to be able to share information effectively to win.
